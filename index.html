The problem

Someone 500 metres away already knows.

The information exists. It is sitting in a WhatsApp group you are not in, or with a neighbour you have not spoken to today. By the time it reaches you, the window where it would have changed your evening has closed.

The estate WhatsApp group A Telegram channel X A Facebook group The security guard Word of mouth Local radio

It is scattered

Seven places to check, and the one that has it is the one you are not in.

It arrives late

A message forwarded through three groups reaches you after you have already driven into the traffic.

It does not know where you are

No group chat can answer the only question that matters: is this relevant to me, right now?

How it works

Report it, confirm it, reach the people it affects.

01

Someone reports it

Two taps. Pick a category, check the location the app already filled in, send. A description is optional, because the category and the place carry most of the value.

Under fifteen seconds
02

The people nearby confirm it

Anyone inside the alert area is asked a direct question: can you see this? Structured answers, not a comment thread. Confidence rises, or the report quietly dies.

No arguing, no replies
03

Only the right people are told

Distance, direction, category and confidence together decide who gets a notification. Someone driving toward a blocked road needs to know. Someone at home behind it does not.

Relevance, not radius
Trust

The hard part is knowing what to believe.

A local alert app is worthless the moment people cannot tell a real incident from something somebody made up. So every alert carries its confidence on its face, everywhere it appears: in the feed, on the map, and inside the notification.

One person cannot ring 180 phones.

An unconfirmed report is visible in the feed and nowhere else. It becomes a notification only once someone else nearby independently confirms it. False alarms cost the sender reach, not the neighbourhood its sleep.

Notifications

If everything is urgent, nothing is.

Notification fatigue is the thing most likely to kill an app like this. So there is a floor and a ceiling. You control almost everything, but not quite everything.

Interrupts you

Critical

Cannot be switched off. Capped at four a day, however many are reported.

  • Confirmed fire
  • Security, once corroborated
  • Severe flooding
Notifies, batched

Important

Grouped, at most one an hour. Forty-seven outage reports make one notification.

  • Roads blocked or closed
  • Power and water
  • Missing person or pet
No notification

For the feed

There when you look, silent when you do not.

  • Community notices
  • Events and closures
  • Lost and found
Privacy

Location is the most sensitive thing you own.

An app that watches where you are has to be unusually specific about what it keeps. The rule is the minimum needed to decide what to show you, and nothing beyond it.

Approximate, never exact

Your rough area, about 500 metres across. Enough to sort alerts by distance, not enough to place you on a street.

Pinned places stay private

Save home, work, a parent's house. None of it is published, and nobody can tell that anyone has pinned a particular address.

Nobody sees where you are

Other people see that an alert is near them. They never see a person on a map. Reports are pinned to the nearest junction, not to whoever sent them.

Off means off

Turn location off and nothing is stored. Follow a place by name instead and the app still works.
