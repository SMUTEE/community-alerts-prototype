Community Alerts System · home screen · three directions

Pick a direction.

Same information architecture, same content, three different arguments about what a community alert app should feel like. Switch the state below. Most of the time this app has nothing to report, and a home screen that only works when something is on fire is not finished.

Direction A

Status

Answers the question before you read anything. An ambient hero that changes colour with the state of your area, then your places, then the detail.

Best at
Working when nothing is happening, which is most evenings. The hero is never empty, so the app always has something to say.
Costs you
The hero eats a third of the screen. On a genuinely busy night you see two alerts before scrolling.
Serves
Risk 3, insufficient density. Calm reads as working, not broken.
Lineage
Apple Weather ↗Jomo ↗Hers ↗

Direction B

Signal

Editorial and dark. One live alert gets hero treatment, everything else is a tight stream where distance is the left margin and severity is a colour tick.

Best at
Scale and speed. Scans in one pass, holds twelve alerts without feeling heavy, and is legible at night, when outages and security incidents actually happen.
Costs you
Dark plus red is one design decision away from alarming. The hero has to be chosen by the system, and it will sometimes choose wrong.
Serves
Risk 1, notification fatigue. The visual hierarchy does the triage the notification tiers cannot.
Lineage
Finimize ↗HYPE ↗Ultrahuman ↗

Direction C

Proximity

Map first. Relevance is shown rather than described. You see that the flooding is between you and the main road, and the sheet rises to meet how much is going on.

Best at
Making location feel like a first-class part of the model, exactly as the PRD argues. Direction and betweenness are free, with no copy needed.
Costs you
A map asks the reader to interpret; a list tells them. Heavier on data and battery, and weakest on the cheap phones and thin connections this is built for.
Serves
The relevance problem in §21, but it contradicts §27, which says the map should not become the product.
Lineage
Tesla Robotaxi ↗Too Good To Go ↗Meetup ↗

If you want a recommendation

B, with A's hero as its quiet state. Signal is the one that still works on the night four things are happening at once, and it is the only one of the three that would make someone say the app looks good. Its real weakness is the opposite case, an empty dark screen that reads as a dead app, and that is exactly what Direction A's ambient hero is for.

C is the most distinctive and the riskiest. It is the right answer if you believe people will open this app while moving, driving home or walking to the gate. It is the wrong answer if they mostly open it sitting down after a notification, which is what the notification-first model in the audit assumes.
